## Slide 05: Causes (part 1)
*key_points* · French Revolution

- Social conditions were extremely unequal and exploitative
- Clergy and nobility formed the first two estates, were privileged, and exempt from paying taxes
- Third estate comprised peasants and workers, formed majority, bore heavy taxes and had no political or social rights
- The estates became extremely disconnected from each other
- Economic strain from wars and American aid
- Tax system favored privileged classes

## Slide 06: Causes (part 2)
*key_points* · French Revolution

- Economic hardship from wars and an unfair tax system caused widespread suffering
- Political cause: Louis XVI was autocratic and weak‑willed, increasing instability
- Discontent among the masses living in extreme poverty and hunger
- French Enlightenment thinkers rejected divine‑right theory
- Philosophers such as Rousseau opposed absolute monarchy
- Inequality of man and sovereignty
- The people actively exposed political system faults and articulated popular discontent

## Slide 07: Stages (part 1)
*process_flow* · French Revolution

- [process]
  1. Meeting of the Estates General (May 5)
  2. Third estate mobilized for equal voting rights
  3. Third estate forms National Assembly (June 17, 1789)
  4. Tennis Court Oath taken at the Tennis Court
  5. Louis XVI absorbs three assemblies, starting the Revolution
  6. Declaration of Rights of Man
- Middle class argued for merit‑based status, not birth
- Nobles opposed fiscal and judicial reforms that threatened their privileges
- Closure ignored meeting’s purpose and king’s authority, preserving traditional system
