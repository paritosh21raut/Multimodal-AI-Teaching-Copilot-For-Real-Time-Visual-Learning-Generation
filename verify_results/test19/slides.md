## Slide 01: Coordination Compounds
*definition* · Chemistry

- **Coordination compounds**: Molecular compounds that retain their identity in both solid and dissolved states, with a central metal atom or ion linked to ions or molecules by coordinate bonds
- [example] potassium ferrocyanide

## Slide 02: Double Salts
*definition* · Chemistry

- **Double salts**: addition molecular compounds that are stable in solid but dissociate into Fe2+, NH4+, and SO4²⁻ ions in solution
- [example] Morse salt
- Morse salt dissociates into Fe2+, NH4+, and SO4²⁻ ions in solution

## Slide 03: Complex Ions (part 1)
*concept* · Chemistry

- **Complex ion**: An electrically charged species where a central metal atom or ion is surrounded by a number of ions or neutral molecules
- Terms related to coordination compounds, the complex ion or coordination entity
- NiCl2·H2O4: Ni is the central metal atom, typically a transition metal
- **Cationic complex entity**: The complex ion that carries a positive charge
- **Anionic complex entity**: The complex ion that carries a negative charge, e.g., FeCN₆⁴⁻
- **Central atom or ion**: The atom or ion to which a fixed number of ions or groups are bound; also called a Lewis acid

## Slide 04: Complex Ions (part 2)
*concept* · Chemistry

- **Ligand**: Electron‑donating species that bonds to the central atom in a coordination entity
- **Monodentate ligand**: Ligand that has one donor site and bonds to the metal ion
- **Bidentate ligand**: Ligand that has two donor sites
- **Polydentate ligand**: Ligand that has several donor sites
- Ligands may be charged or neutral
- [example] H₂O; NH₃

## Slide 05: Complex Ions (part 3)
*concept* · Chemistry

- **Ambidentate ligand**: Monodentate ligand that can bind through two different donor atoms
- **Chelating ligand**: Polydentate ligand that uses two or more donor atoms to stabilize a metal atom
- **Chelate effect**: Stabilization of coordination compounds due to chelation
- **Coordination number**: Number of coordinate bonds formed by the central metal atom with the ligands
- **Coordination sphere**: The central metal ion and its attached ligands enclosed in square brackets
- The ionos-sable group written outside the bracket is known as counter-ions

## Slide 15: Theories of Coordination Compounds (part 2)
*members* · Chemistry

- **Wannesbord theory**: Proposed by L. Pauling in the 1930s; metal ion supplies empty orbitals to surrounding ligands
- Coordination number equals the number of empty metal orbitals
- These empty orbitals hybridize to form bonds
- Hybridization depends on the metal and the approaching ligands

## Slide 16: Inner and Outer Orbital Complexes
*concept* · Chemistry

- **Outer orbital complex**: Complex that uses outer d orbitals and the d shell for bonding
- **Inner orbital complex**: Complex that uses inner d orbitals and the d shell for bonding
- Formed with weak or high‑spin ligands
- Hybridization type is sp³d²
- Octahedral geometry with six ligands
- Cannot explain quantized magnetic data
- Cannot explain existence of inner/outer orbital complexes
- Cannot explain temperature‑dependent magnetic moments
- Cannot explain colors of complexes

## Slide 17: Crystal Field Theory (part 1)
*definition* · Chemistry

- **Crystal field theory (CFT)**: Theory proposed by H. Bethwick in 1929 to explain bonding in coordination compounds
- Van Vleck and Orgel applied CFT to coordination compounds in 1935
- In CFT ligands are modeled as point charges for anionic ligands and as dipoles for neutral ligands
- The five d orbitals are classified; dx, dy, dz lie between the coordinate axes
- Other two d orbitals are d x²‑y² and d z² (e_g orbitals)

## Slide 18: Crystal Field Theory (part 2)
*key_points* · Chemistry

- Ligand approach splits the five degenerate d orbitals; the extent of splitting depends on the crystal field
- D x square minus y square and d z square oriented along the axis is called the CG orbitals

- **Crystal field splitting energy**: Energy difference between T₂g and E_g levels, designated by Δ
