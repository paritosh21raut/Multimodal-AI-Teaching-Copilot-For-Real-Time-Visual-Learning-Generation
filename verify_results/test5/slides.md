## Slide 01: S-Block Elements (part 1)
*concept* · Chemistry

- **S-block elements**: the last electron enters in the s orbital
- Two groups (1 and 2) belong to the s‑block because the s orbital holds only two electrons
- S‑block elements are not found in nature as free atoms
- Occur in the narrative or free state
- **General electronic configuration of s‑block elements**: ns¹ or ns²
- **Alkali metals**: Group 1 elements have one electron in their valence shell
- Alkali metals form strong alkalis (e.g., NaOH, KOH)

## Slide 03: Alkali Metals Characteristics (part 1)
*key_points* · Chemistry

- One valence electron (ns¹)
- Largest atomic radius in each period
- Atomic radius increases down the group
- The alkyl metals have the biggest atomic radar in their respective periods
- Ionic radius is smaller because alkali metals lose electron shells
- Fewer shells and higher nuclear charge cause a contractive effect
- Ionic radius of alkali metal ions increases down the group
- Light metals have low densities
- Alkali metals: Bcc lattice, coordination number 8

## Slide 04: Alkali Metals Characteristics (part 2)
*key_points* · Chemistry

- Lithium is the lightest metal
- Density rises down the group because atomic mass increases more than atomic size
- Potassium density is lower than sodium because its atomic size increases abnormally when moving down the group
- Melting and boiling points are low and decrease down the group
- Weak metallic bonding causes low melting points
- Francium is a liquid at room temperature

## Slide 05: Alkali Metals Characteristics (part 3)
*key_points* · Chemistry

- Soft, mirrorable, ductile solids that can be cut with a knife
- Metallic luster
- Shine when freshly cut due to isolation of electrons
- Atomic volume highest in each period
- Atomic volume increases down the group
- First ionization enthalpy lowest in period
- First ionization enthalpy decreases down the group
- Second ionization enthalpy very high

## Slide 06: Alkali Metals Characteristics (part 4)
*key_points* · Chemistry

- Second ionization is difficult because removal breaks noble‑gas configuration and due to low electropositive character
- Electropositive nature increases down the group as ionization enthalpy decreases
