=== 1. THE PROJECT ===
"Multimodal AI Teaching Copilot for Real-Time Visual Learning Generation". A laptop app listens to a teacher's live
lecture through a microphone and shows a live educational display on the classroom projector. Nothing is prepared
in advance: no script, no topic list, no slides given beforehand. Everything comes from live speech.

It is also a research project: each version is meant to become an IEEE conference paper, so the approach needs a
clear novelty (e.g. "open-vocabulary, continuity-aware scene graph from live speech + exact law solvers", not
"we called an image API").

Versions:
- V1 (DONE, works live): live slides. Speech -> text -> LLM understanding -> a deterministic planner builds and

- V2 (CURRENT, STUCK): "Concept Simulation / Canvas for Imagination" - draw and animate what the teacher says.
- V3 (NEXT): "Story Scenes" - live narration becomes a moving storyboard.
- V4 (later): multilingual lectures reusing the same core.

=== 2. MY VISION FOR V2: "CANVAS FOR IMAGINATION" ===
When the teacher describes something, it appears on the slide within ~2 seconds as a clean, animated,
explainer-quality scene - as if a Kurzgesagt, TED-Ed, 3Blue1Brown or Veritasium animator were drawing live along
with the teacher. Not stickers on a slide; a real scene that acts.

Examples of what the teacher might say, and what should appear:
- "A man pushes a box across a rough floor" -> a man in a pushing pose, box sliding, friction and applied force
  arrows (each its own colour), the box decelerating correctly when he stops pushing.
- "Rain falls vertically, but a man walking forward feels it slanted" -> walking man, rain, then the same scene
  "seen from the man", with velocity vectors; the slant angle computed, not guessed.
- "An electron orbits the nucleus... now focus on the electron" -> atom animation, camera zooms to the electron.
- "Throw a book in the air" -> a character actually throws it; exact projectile path drawn as a dotted line.
- "If you jumped on the Moon and then on Mars" -> two side-by-side jumps with correct heights for each gravity.
- "Split the area under the curve into thin strips" -> 3Blue1Brown-style integration strips that get thinner.
- "A lightning rod protects a building", "the solar system", "two cars collide", "a wave on a string",
  "electric and magnetic fields of a wave".
- Odd, non-educational ones too: "a bicycle under the ocean", "a laptop flying on top of an airplane".

Required qualities:
- OPEN VOCABULARY: anything said - things, places, people, animals, actions, relations ("on", "under",
  "holding", "behind"). No hard-coded topic scenes.
- ONE CONSISTENT STYLE per lecture, textbook/explainer quality. Style itself is fully open (flat 2D, 3D, realistic,
  vector) - whatever works best. Characters must not drift (same face, same body) across frames and scenes.
- REAL ACTIONS: characters walk, run, push, pull, throw, hold, point, wave, think; live faces (blink, gaze,
  expressions, talking). Objects have correct relative real-world sizes but stay readable.
- CORRECT PHYSICS: the LLM only states WHAT is in the scene; deterministic Python law solvers (kinematics,
  projectile, relative motion, forces/friction, collisions, fields, waves, rigid bodies, orbits) compute HOW it
  moves and every number shown. Never display a number the teacher didn't say or code didn't compute.
- CONTINUITY: the scene evolves as the teacher continues ("now he throws it harder", "add a second car").
- CAMERA: any angle, focus/zoom on a part, by voice or by teacher click on a control page.
- Drawn on the slide itself (whole slide = canvas), never a separate screen. "Imagine Mode" button: every line is
  drawn until the teacher presses End, then slides resume.
- Safety filter for inappropriate content.

=== 3. V3 VISION: "STORY SCENES" ===
When the teacher narrates ("In 1905 a young clerk in Bern...", "A drop of water starts in the ocean...",
"Imagine you are a red blood cell..."), a storyboard plays along with the narration: scenes with captions only from
the said words, camera pans/zooms, transitions, and the SAME characters and places carried across scenes. It reuses
V2's scene graph, assets, renderer and continuity. So whatever asset/character system V2 uses must also support
multi-scene stories with consistent characters.

=== 6. WHAT WE HAVE BUILT AND TRIED (in order) ===
a) 21 hand-built simulation TEMPLATES (incline, pendulum, circuits, waves, orbits, Bohr atom, heart, pH, ...),
   auto-triggered by cue words. Accurate and live-verified, but only covers pre-built topics. Not imagination.
b) Scene core with topic "worlds" (geometry, plots, motion, relative motion) + trigger words. Accurate, but
   anything outside the coded worlds falls through. Still restricts imagination.
c) Open canvas: LLM turns each line into scene ops (add thing/place, relation, action), rendered with 3D emoji,
   photo cutouts, backdrops, a real-size table, focus camera, Imagine Mode, safety filter. Runs live (line ->
   first shape p50 2.1 s). RESULT: looks like separate stickers pasted on a slide. Each picture has one fixed pose,
   styles are mixed (emoji next to photos), a person cannot actually throw or push, wrong word sense sometimes,
   photo search ~5 s cold.
d) Image generation: SD-Turbo locally = 0.41 s/image alone but 11.6 s while Whisper shares the GPU; ignores style
   prompts; every image looks different; the same character changes between frames; cannot give consistent
   animation frames. Cloud free tiers looked at but too slow/limited for per-line generation.
e) Style board: 7 code-drawn styles (stick figure, chalk, flat vector, ...). All rejected as not good enough.
   We then made reference sheets (characters in all poses, body parts, face expressions, animals, objects) with a
   chatbot image generator and chose that flat style.
f) Character built in code as a flat-unlit 3D model in three.js (spheres/cylinders + canvas-texture live face),
   with clips (walk, run, push, throw, wave, point, think), IK, physics arrows, dotted projectile path.
   Motion, arrows and live expressions were GOOD (144 fps, 2 ms/frame). Face, hair and body looked "very bad";
   a second round of tuning made it worse. Hand-shaping 3D primitives to match 2D art does not converge.
g) CURRENT: 2D cut-out puppets cut from the reference sheets (each body part a separate image, rigged and animated
   by our engine, faces from expression sheets), 3D world kept for physics and camera. The side view looks like
   the sheet. BUT every view (front, side, back, top, 8 angles) needs its own matching part sheets; the AI-made
   sheets drift in scale, colour and proportions, their joint data is wrong, so each part needs manual measuring.
   And this is one man - we would need women, children, animals, vehicles, thousands of objects.

=== 7. WHY WE THINK WE KEEP FAILING ===
- Coverage vs consistency: "anything" needs a picture of anything in any pose in one style. Fixed assets (emoji,
  photos, templates) are consistent-ish but limited; generated images cover anything but are inconsistent and slow.
  Nothing so far gives both.
- Animation needs rigged, multi-view assets. Photos, emoji and AI images are single frames - movable, not actable.
- No artist or 3D modeller on the team, no rigged asset library; code-made art looks amateur.
- 4 GB VRAM shared with live STT; free LLM quota; ~2 s latency budget rules out heavy generation per line.
- Ambiguous language and STT errors (e.g. "spear" heard as "keyboard"); odd combinations have no reference.
- The quality bar ("explainer video" look) is high and subjective: each round passes tests but fails the eye.
What works well already: speech -> LLM scene ops, the physics solvers, the camera, arrows/trajectories, the
deterministic core, and live code-drawn faces/motion. What is missing is the visual ASSET + ANIMATION layer.
