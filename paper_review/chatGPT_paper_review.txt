The final issues I would fix
1. Resolve the “extend-only” contradiction
This is the most important remaining issue because it concerns the central contribution.
The abstract says the planner enforces extend-only operations that cannot rewrite previously displayed content. However, Section III-E explains that shown text can change through composer merges, mishearing corrections and teacher edits. Table V then reports that 91 statements were rewritten or removed in the proposed system. IEEE_Paper_Multimodal_AI_Teaching_Copilot_v4.pdf
IEEE_Paper_Multimodal_AI_Teaching_Copilot_v4.pdf
IEEE_Paper_Multimodal_AI_Teaching_Copilot_v4.pdf

You can address this without changing your architecture. Make the distinction explicit:
- The LLM cannot freely regenerate existing slides.
- The planner restricts slide-update operations.
- Limited changes can still arise from composer merges, corrections and teacher edits, and these are measured separately.
I recommend changing the abstract's strict claim to something like “a constrained slide-update policy that prevents unrestricted LLM rewrites of previously displayed content.” This is more precise than claiming that displayed content can never change.
2. Align the baseline conclusion with what the experiment proves
Your new baseline experiment is a meaningful improvement. However, the baseline removes not only the planner but also the guards and composer, while the proposed system uses all of them. The comparison therefore demonstrates that the overall proposed system reduces rewrites relative to the tested baseline; it does not isolate the causal effect of the extend-only policy by itself. The discussion acknowledges that an append-only baseline prompt was not tested. IEEE_Paper_Multimodal_AI_Teaching_Copilot_v4.pdf
IEEE_Paper_Multimodal_AI_Teaching_Copilot_v4.pdf

For the strongest paper, add a direct append-only baseline or report rewrite rates for the relevant replay ablation. If time does not permit another experiment, retain the results but narrow the claims to the specific baseline you tested.
Also ensure the denominator for each rewrite percentage is defined consistently. The current reported totals differ between systems, so readers need to understand precisely how each rate is calculated. IEEE_Paper_Multimodal_AI_Teaching_Copilot_v4.pdf

3. Correct the rating range in the abstract
The abstract says teachers rated the slides from 3.9 to 4.2 out of 5. Table III's overall column reports means between 3.87 and 4.17, while individual subgroup values range from 3.76 to 4.26. IEEE_Paper_Multimodal_AI_Teaching_Copilot_v4.pdf
IEEE_Paper_Multimodal_AI_Teaching_Copilot_v4.pdf

A precise replacement would be:
“Across the four rating criteria, overall mean scores ranged from 3.87 to 4.17 out of 5.”

That describes the overall column without implying that every subgroup value lies within those bounds.
4. Make the results reproducible
The paper is already more transparent about its dataset, thresholds, model routing, annotation procedure and statistical tests. Before submission, verify that the underlying logs support every reported number, particularly the paired baseline test and planner ablation.
I also recommend providing direct links to the six public lectures, the annotation guidelines, and the scripts or anonymized evaluation data where permission allows. Keep the single-lecture WER result clearly scoped to the one lecture on which it was measured. IEEE_Paper_Multimodal_AI_Teaching_Copilot_v4.pdf

The model identifier qwen/qwen3.8-27b is listed in Groq's official model documentation, so its spelling is not inherently an error; nevertheless, document the exact model and configuration used for your experiments, as hosted models and versions can change.

The remaining issue is not that your paper lacks results. It is that the main contribution must be described exactly as the implementation and experiments support it. A selective IEEE conference could still question novelty, the baseline design, or the lack of classroom learning-outcome evaluation.
Final submission recommendation
Make one more focused revision, then consider submitting. Prioritize the extend-only wording, narrow the baseline claim or add a direct append-only comparison, and correct the abstract's rating range. Verify the results against your logs and do a final template check for your target venue.
After those changes, I would consider the manuscript ready for a serious submission attempt, subject to the specific conference or journal's scope and requirements. Acceptance will still depend on peer review.
